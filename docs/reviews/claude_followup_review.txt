# Follow-up audit of `ooh_pred` after the stratified-split revision

## 1. Verdict

**Ready, with three small fixes and one disclosure. I found no defect that changes a reported number.** Every revision listed in `response.md` is in the code and in the outputs. I recomputed the headline numbers by a separate route and they match.

Three things are still worth fixing before circulation:

- **The full-versus-reduced comparison depends on which split you draw.** I checked this by comparing the old and new splits.
- **The multiple-placement model's probabilities are no more accurate than a constant guess,** even though it ranks youth well. The paper doesn't say this.
- **One axis in the calibration figure is mislabelled:** the right panel prints 2.5% and 7.5% as "2%" and "8%".

Unlike the previous review, I could run R this time. I ran read-only checks on the saved derived files and on the aggregate tables, and wrote only temporary page images to `/tmp`. I did not rerun the pipeline or edit anything.

## 2. Ranked findings

**F1. Split variability is larger than the paper's intervals suggest, and the full-versus-reduced reading depends on the split (checked; inferential).**

`docs/reviews/pre_stratification_performance.csv` holds the results from the earlier random split; `tabs/performance.csv` holds the new stratified split.

| Metric | Old split | New split (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|
| Placement PR-AUC, full model | 0.191 | 0.211 |
| Placement PR-AUC, CANS + demographics | 0.207 | 0.165 |
| Placement Brier score, full model | 0.0522 | 0.0515 |
| Placement Brier score, CANS + demographics | 0.0519 | 0.0528 |
| Multiple-placement PR-AUC, full model | 0.093 | 0.050 (0.030 to 0.089) |

- **What changed:** on PR-AUC and Brier score, the ordering of the two placement models flips between splits. The old multiple-placement PR-AUC lies outside the new interval.
- **Why that matters:** the paper's intervals hold the trained model and the split fixed. Moving from one 80/20 split to another moves estimates by more than those intervals cover.
- **What the paper already says:** it states this limitation in general terms (`ms/main.Rmd:147`, `:343`) and claims no significant paired difference.
- **What is still exposed:** the sentence at `ms/main.Rmd:271` sets one split's point estimates side by side, and readers will take the ordering as informative.
- **Fix:** report the full-versus-reduced difference across repeated stratified splits (say 20–50, or nested cross-validation), or add a sentence saying the ordering is not stable across splits.
- **Hypothesis, not tested:** I did not run repeated splits, so I can't say how wide the across-split spread is. Note also that the two runs differ in the split *and* in the internal fold assignment, so I can't separate their effects.

**F2. The multiple-placement model ranks well but its probabilities are no better than a constant (checked; interpretation not stated).**

- *Brier skill* is the share of the constant guess's Brier score that the model removes. The constant guess predicts the training event rate for every youth.
- From `tabs/repeat_placement_baselines.csv`, the Brier score is 0.011521 for the lasso and 0.011600 for the constant guess.
- On the saved test predictions, with 2,000 person-level resamples:
  - Multiple placements: Brier skill **0.7% (95% CI −2.4% to 3.0%)**.
  - Placement: Brier skill **7.8% (4.7% to 10.9%)**.
- Table 4 shows 0.012 against 0.012, but the prose (`main.Rmd:189–191`, "shows predictive signal") never says the gain is in *ranking* only (ROC AUC 0.85), not in probability accuracy.
- **Fix:** add one sentence.

**F3. Calibration-figure tick labels are wrong in the right panel (checked).**

- I rebuilt the plot in memory with `ggplot_build`. The right panel's ticks sit at 0, 0.025, 0.05 and 0.075, but are labelled "0%, 2%, 5%, 8%".
- The cause is `scales::label_percent(accuracy = 1)` at `scripts/figures.R:30–31`, which rounds 2.5% and 7.5% to whole percentages. It is visible on rendered page 9.
- The caption's claim that horizontal and vertical ranges match within each panel is true: both axes run from −0.0045 to 0.0945 on the right and −0.015 to 0.315 on the left.
- Cosmetic side issue: the fixed error-bar `width = 0.004` draws heavy black blocks near zero in the rare-event panel.
- **Fix:** use `accuracy = 0.1` or set the breaks explicitly.

**F4. Minor editorial points (checked).**

- **365-day multiple-placement result:** its ROC AUC of 0.901, the largest number in Table 8, rests on 16 test events. Its sensitivity interval in `performance.csv` is degenerate (1.000 [1.000, 1.000]). The prose flags only the 180-day result, which has 7 events.
- **Reduced model for multiple placements:** it has higher PR-AUC (0.057 vs 0.050) and lower Brier score (0.0113 vs 0.0115) than the full model, but lower ROC AUC (0.797 vs 0.849). `main.Rmd:271` discusses placement only.
- **Table 6 repeats Tables 7–8.** Its AUC columns duplicate theirs. Table 6 could keep only occupied days.
- **Leftovers from the previous review:**
  - Dates still render with leading zeros ("January 03, 2011").
  - `2016-11-30` is hard-coded in `scripts/estimate.R:26`. It equals the last discharge date plus one day (699 = `last_discharge_horizon`), but nothing asserts that.

## 5. Remaining scientific limits (all disclosed)

- **Selected cohort:** the sample was chosen partly using prediction-window events, so results describe discrimination within this cohort only.
- **Incomplete ascertainment:** a missing admission record is not proof of no placement.
- **No historical snapshots** of the predictors.
- **Narrow intervals:** they hold the trained model and split fixed, and ignore dependence within families. F1 is concrete evidence that this matters.
- **Optional extensions, not demands:** prior-placement predictors; subgroup calibration if the model is ever used operationally.
